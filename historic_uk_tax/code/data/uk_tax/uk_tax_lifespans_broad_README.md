# UK Tax Lifespans Broad Dataset

This is the first working dataset for a broad historical count of UK taxes. As at the 2026-05-29 O'Brien wartime-tax cross-check it contains 369 tax/levy episodes across 339 `tax_id`s.

The current/announced endpoint contains 90 counted rows with `episode_status` of `active` or `not_yet_in_force`: 86 currently active rows plus four future/announced rows. The current income-tax chart leaves (work, dividend, savings and rent income tax) are counted separately to match the sunburst terminal-slice convention. Residual aggregate rows such as `other_taxes_nes` and `current_small_tax_residual` are marked `chart_split_only` and are not counted as individual taxes.

The count chart generated by `code/uk/build_tax_lifespan_count_chart.py` starts at 1000 by default, the earliest currently dated row (`fumage`).

The lifespan timeline chart generated by `code/uk/build_tax_lifespan_timeline_chart.py` sorts tax episodes by start date and colours them by broad category. Rows marked `chart_split_only` are skipped rather than counted as separate taxes.

## Counting Unit

Each row is an episode of a separately identifiable tax, duty, levy, licence duty, assessment or analogous compulsory public charge. Re-enactments after repeal should be separate episodes sharing the same `tax_name` where appropriate.

The broad series intentionally counts granular duties where a contemporary source treats them separately, for example beer, malt and hops; hearth-money and window tax; separate profession licences; and commodity duties such as glass, soap, paper, printed-goods duties and salt.

Dates for older taxes should be read as best current coding rather than final statutory certainty. Many medieval and early-modern taxes were granted episodically, reworked by later consolidating Acts, or described by Dowell as already existing without a clean start date. Where Dowell proves a duty was in force but does not identify the original enactment, the dataset uses a "known in force by" start year and marks the row as low-confidence. Where a broad older duty was plainly absorbed into a later consolidated regime, the endpoint may be approximate and should be checked against the underlying statute before relying on the precise year.

## Key Fields

- `tax_id`: stable identifier for the tax concept.
- `episode`: integer episode number for repeat enactments.
- `start_year` / `end_year`: every counted row has a start year; every non-active counted row has an end year, including low-confidence best guesses where needed.
- `episode_status`: `ended`, `active`, `not_yet_in_force`, or `chart_split_only`.
- `date_confidence`: `high`, `medium`, `low`, or `placeholder`.
- `source_id`: links to `uk_tax_lifespans_broad_sources.tsv`.
- `source_quote`: short supporting quote or OCR-derived excerpt. OCR spelling has not been silently corrected in quotations.

## Current Limitations

This is not yet complete. It is a structured seed that captures the obvious historical backbone from Dowell, Parliament's key dates, the current chart, HMRC/ONS modern workbooks, official web sources for modern/devolved taxes, and statute-level passes through selected Finance Acts, Local Government Acts, land-value/development-land legislation and related repeal provisions.

Open work:

- Verify start/end dates for current taxes not yet supported by specific legislation or official guidance.
- Continue post-1885 replacements and repeals for the small number of duties that Dowell records only as extant in 1885, especially dried-fruit duties and rum duty. These now have 1885 best-guess endpoints rather than open-ended status.
- Verify the 1787 approximate endpoints now applied to early customs/impost lineages: wool customs, tunnage/poundage, tobacco impost and currants impost.
- The dataset currently excludes the broad `customs`, `excise_duties` and `stamp_duties` umbrella rows from the count while separately counting named commodity, excise and stamp heads. This avoids obvious parent/child double-counting but means the early-modern commodity-duty coverage still needs continuing expansion.
- Improve the local-rate series beyond the current broad poor-rate/general-rate/business-rate lineage, especially Scotland, Ireland/Northern Ireland, cesses and special local rates.
- Finish section-level checks for boundary cases added in the second statute pass: development charge, betterment levy, tax on development gains, advance corporation tax, munitions levy and armament profits duty.
- Decide the final boundary for ONS-classified tax-like fees and levies, such as licence fees, visa fees, Renewables Obligation, Contracts for Difference and other levy-funded bodies.
- Resolve remaining audit boundary choices: table waters duty is currently ended in 1926 rather than 1924; Advance PRT is ended in 1986 rather than 1987; Composite Rate Tax is started in 1951 rather than 1985 because the building-society regime predates the bank-deposit extension.
- Several current chart-leaf rows split broader statutory families, for example NICs, corporation tax and SDLT. They are retained for revenue-chart traceability but marked `chart_split_only`, so they do not count separately in the historical series. Income-tax leaves are the exception: work, dividend, savings and rent income tax are counted separately to keep the endpoint consistent with the current article/chart convention.
- The old-list reconciliations added prisage of wine, apprenticeship indentures duty, Dashwood's cider/perry tax, imported eggs duty, soda/potash/aerated waters duty, wallpaper/stained-paper duty, chip/straw/cane/horsehair hats duty, cochineal duty, several William III poll-tax episodes, split printed-goods duties, the 1784 cotton-stuffs duty, Triple Assessment, Shadow ACT, and a further old_taxes3 pass of Commonwealth excise heads and old customs/property episodes. They exclude pre-UK/Roman items, feudal incidents, proposed taxes, umbrellas, residual buckets and one-off royal fines unless separately reclassified after source review.
- Some current ONS/OBR fee and residual lines have low-confidence start dates or aggregate placeholders, especially `levy_funded_bodies`, `other_taxes_nes` and `current_small_tax_residual`. These should be disaggregated into named statutory levies where possible.
- The O'Brien WP99 wartime-tax cross-check added three low-confidence minor French-war rows (`silk_thread_lace_war_duty`, `ships_hulls_materials_duty`, `madder_duty`) and moved the start dates for `flax_duty`, `indigo_duty` and `tallow_duty` from "known in force by 1815" to earlier French-war dates. The exact enactment/repeal provisions for the three new minor rows remain to be traced.

Known exclusions/merged rows:

- The 1547/1548 sheep-and-cloth charge in 2 & 3 Edw. VI c.36 is treated as a component of the Tudor subsidy row, not as a separate tax. Dowell describes it as part of a subsidy Act that also charged goods, with the sheep and cloth charge cancelled the next year.
